📚 Literature Review: Data Visualisation for Economic Crisis Prediction

Comprehensive literature review examining the evolution and application of data visualisation techniques in economic crisis prediction. This review spans research from 1998 to 2025, analyzing traditional statistical approaches, interactive visualisation tools, machine learning integration, and real-world institutional applications by the IMF and ESRB. The review critically evaluates the interpretability-accuracy trade-off in predictive models and explores how visual analytics can enhance early warning systems for financial crises.

Key Topics Covered:

  • Evolution of Visualisation: From simple threshold-based systems (Kaminsky et al., 1998) to complex ML-enhanced frameworks
  • Main Techniques: Time-series charts, heat maps, interactive dashboards, and network visualisations for systemic risk
  • Machine Learning Integration: ROC curves, feature importance, dimensionality reduction (PCA, t-SNE), and interpretability tools
  • Institutional Applications: IMF Financial Soundness Indicators dashboard and ESRB risk monitoring systems
  • Critical Evaluation: Limitations of rare event prediction, cognitive biases, and validation challenges in crisis detection
  • Research Gaps: Need for emerging data sources integration and improved temporal dynamics visualization

Research Context:

This literature review supports the research proposal for Module 6, examining how visualisation techniques can enhance machine learning model interpretability in financial crisis prediction. The review synthesizes findings from 20+ key studies spanning early warning systems, institutional frameworks, and recent ML research, providing the theoretical foundation for investigating the balance between model accuracy and interpretability through enhanced visual analytics.

⚖️ Exercise 8.3: Diet Effectiveness Comparison

Statistical comparison of two diet programs using relative frequency histograms to analyze weight loss patterns. This exercise explores measures of central tendency (mean), dispersion (standard deviation), and distribution shapes to evaluate intervention effectiveness and consistency across participants. The analysis demonstrates Diet A's superior performance with higher average weight loss (5.3 kg vs 3.7 kg) and more consistent outcomes (SD 2.5 kg vs 2.8 kg).

Key Findings:

  • Diet A: Superior results - Mean 5.3 kg loss, consistent outcomes (SD = 2.5 kg), symmetric bell-shaped distribution
  • Diet B: Lower effectiveness - Mean 3.7 kg loss, higher variability (SD = 2.8 kg), more spread with slight right skew
  • Reliability: Diet A shows predictable outcomes with nearly all participants losing weight (minimum = -1.7 kg)
  • Diet B Variability: Some participants gained weight (minimum = -4.1 kg), peak frequency at 2 kg indicates modest results for many
  • Recommendation: Diet A preferred due to higher average results and consistent, predictable outcomes

📝 Reflective Analysis: Key Themes and Learning

This reflective analysis examines my learning progression through Module 6: Research Methods and Professional Practice. In contrast to previous technical modules focusing on AI implementations, this module deepened my understanding of how research is conducted, how knowledge claims are justified, and how ethical considerations permeate every aspect of the research process.

Key Reflective Themes:

  • Research Fundamentals: Moving from technical AI implementation to understanding research design, ethics, and methodology
  • Statistical Interpretation: Learning that statistical analysis requires not just calculation but meaningful interpretation for decision-making
  • Visualisation as Communication: Recognising that charts and dashboards shape how patterns are noticed and conclusions formed
  • Ethical Considerations: Understanding that methodology choices carry ethical weight beyond technical decisions
  • Literature Synthesis: Developing ability to synthesize contradictory evidence and acknowledge uncertainty
  • Interpretability vs Accuracy: Wrestling with the fundamental trade-off in ML models for crisis prediction

Description of Experience:

Module 6 represented a significant shift from technical AI modules to examining the research process itself— learning how to ask the right questions, design detailed literature reviews, and communicate findings effectively. The module bridged fundamental research design with advanced statistical analysis, culminating in developing a comprehensive research proposal on economic crisis prediction using data visualisation techniques. Key experiences included working through ethics case studies (Cambridge Analytica, automated weaponry), completing inferential statistics exercises (hypothesis testing, summary measures), creating effective data visualisations (brand preferences, ecological surveys, diet effectiveness), synthesizing literature spanning 1998-2025, and developing an integrated research proposal addressing the interpretability-accuracy trade-off in ML models.

Analysis and Interpretation:

The module transformed my relationship with data and research. Rather than rushing to analysis, I learned to slow down and question fundamental assumptions: Why this test? What biases might exist? How might sampling introduce error? The ethical case studies demonstrated that research methodology isn't neutral—every choice about measurement and presentation carries ethical weight. The statistics work moved beyond technical competence to translation and communication, particularly through visualisation exercises that forced thinking about how data tells stories. The literature review exposed me to nuanced, contradictory evidence without clear consensus, requiring comfort with uncertainty rather than premature conclusions. The research proposal revealed the persistent interpretability-accuracy dilemma: the most accurate models are least transparent, yet stakeholders need to understand predictions to act on them. Enhanced visualisation offers a partial solution, but this remains a genuine trade-off without easy answers.

Application and Future Development:

Moving forward, several changes are needed in my approach to research and data analysis. First, maintaining critical stance toward statistical outputs—questioning sample characteristics, measurement validity, potential confounds, and alternative explanations. Second, developing systematic expertise in visualisation tools like Power BI, studying both technical aspects and design principles that make visualisations effective. Third, keeping ethical dimensions central by regularly asking "who might be harmed?" and "what am I not seeing?" when working on data projects. Fourth, focusing on understanding evidence quality and applying it appropriately in selected methodologies—crucial for financial risk management where decisions must be made with incomplete information. Finally, improving communication skills to translate complex research for diverse stakeholders (policymakers, business leaders, general audiences) without oversimplifying or losing essential nuance. This module reinforced that research is about developing sound judgement, not just learning techniques—a perspective that will influence my dissertation and broader career engagement with research and data.
